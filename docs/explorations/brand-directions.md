# Staple Cut hero directions

Both previews use the same product illustration, headline, supporting copy, and action. They are visual explorations, not selected storefront art.

## Shared content

- **Product:** the starter's plain white T-shirt illustration (`public/assets/white-shirt.svg`).
- **Headline:** “Your no-thought daily uniform.”
- **Support:** “Staple Cut is designed with a focus on fit and comfort for everyday wear.”
- **Action:** “Explore the concept.”
- **Product framing:** the concept treats fit and comfort as design intent, not measured test results.

## Modernist: order for everyday ease

- **Reference:** Josef Müller-Brockmann, _Musica Viva_, 1957, MoMA collection object 58.1960 — <https://www.moma.org/collection/works/4885>.
- **Observed:** a disciplined type grid occupies the poster's upper area; a small set of diagonal bars creates a measured visual rhythm across the open field.
- **Preview choices:** clear alignment, consistent type hierarchy, a restrained blue accent, and a quiet grid. The diagonal rhythm is echoed as a secondary detail.
- **Intended audience effect:** the everyday concept may feel dependable and easy to scan.
- **Risk:** the spare structure may feel clinical beside the brand's warm voice.

## Postmodernist: play with the everyday

- **Reference:** Ettore Sottsass, _'Carlton' Room Divider_, 1981, MoMA collection object 1290.2018 — <https://www.moma.org/collection/works/292736>.
- **Observed:** familiar furniture is remade from bright, angled planes and a mix of geometric forms.
- **Preview choices:** color-blocked planes frame the same shirt; a tilted label, mixed type voice, and shaped action button add a playful layer.
- **Intended audience effect:** the everyday uniform may feel expressive and memorable while retaining the same promise.
- **Risk:** added shapes may compete with the shirt and the “no-thought” idea.
