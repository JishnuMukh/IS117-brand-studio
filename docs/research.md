Look and character reading (hypotheses): Fresh Clean Tees reads as Everyperson with a joking tone; True Classic reads as Hero (confidence, "look sharper"); Buck Mason reads as Creator or craftsman (provenance, restraint).

## 2. Two possible positions

| | Position A: comfort-first daily uniform | Position B: one plain tee, clear total |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Idea | A tee you stop thinking about: soft, easy, good for frequent casual wear | One tee, one straightforward price, no bundle or discount decisions |
| Contrast with references | Fresh Clean Tees also leads with softness, so the difference would be specificity about everyday situations | Contrasts with True Classic's many pack choices and sale pricing |
| Risk | Comfort claims cannot be verified for a concept product | May feel like less value to bulk buyers |

**Choice (confirm):** Position A, because it matches the Staple Cut promise already in `SPEC.md` ("your no-thought daily uniform", focus on fit and comfort).

## 4. Chosen audience

For people who wear a plain tee on frequent casual days, our Staple Cut white tee helps them choose a comfortable everyday default. We emphasize specific everyday situations and clear fabric and fit information instead of adjectives. We still need to check whether comfort-focused visitors actually want fabric and measurement details up front, and whether the situations (hot-Sunday errands, flights, brunch) feel recognizable to them.

## 5. Brand character and voice

Everyperson archetype. Voice: candid and direct, warm and relatable, confident and assured (all from `SPEC.md`). Example line from the brief: "It's the first shirt you'll reach for on Sunday morning, and the only one you'll want to wear on Tuesday." This is concept copy; customer preference is untested.

Visual direction: modernist (grid and hierarchy, after Josef Müller-Brockmann's *Musica Viva*, 1957), chosen so a comfort-seeking visitor can scan and choose easily. Neutral palette with cobalt accents, one-color interlocking S/C logo.

## 6. Honest persuasion: two applications

| Principle | Where it appears | Limit on the claim |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Unity | "For the days you already have" section, starting with hot-Sunday errands | Specific scenarios may help the visitor recognize their own routine. This shows a design intention, not proof of connection; heat, breathability and fit claims are unverified. |
| Reciprocity | Optional "Free tee checklist" with fabric composition and weight first, then measurements, care and construction | Useful information given first may help the visitor compare options. It does not prove more purchases, and no softness or shape-retention results are verified. |

What I will not copy from the references: unsourced percentages, invented counts of customers or reviews, and a sale presented as scarcity. Staple Cut is a labeled concept store, so it will not show reviews or sales numbers.

## 7. Assumptions to check (next steps)

- Comfort-focused visitors want fabric and fit details early (ask a classmate in the peer review; record what they do, not just what they say).
- The everyday-situation scenarios are recognizable to the primary persona.
- Whether a fit guide with measurements should come next (candidate for the Plan stage), since all three references give fit or sizing help of some kind.
