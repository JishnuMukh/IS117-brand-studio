IS117 Brand Studio Reference and design notes

Staple Cut / visual exploration

Two ways to frame the same tee.

Both previews use the same white T-shirt, promise, supporting copy, and action. Compare how the visual structure changes the feeling of the concept.

01 / Modernist

Order for everyday ease

Open full preview ↗

Reference Josef Müller-Brockmann, Musica Viva, 1957, MoMA object 58.1960. View the poster at MoMA ↗

Choices A strict alignment grid, restrained color, and repeated linear rhythm make the message quick to scan.

Intended effect The comfort-first concept may feel dependable and easy to choose.

Risk The spare structure may feel clinical beside Staple Cut’s warm voice.

02 / Postmodernist

Play with the everyday

Open full preview ↗

Reference Ettore Sottsass, 'Carlton' Room Divider, 1981, MoMA object 1290.2018. View the object at MoMA ↗

Choices Color-blocked planes, tilted frames, and a mix of typographic voices bring play into the product scene.

Intended effect The everyday uniform may feel expressive and memorable without changing the promise.

Risk Extra shapes may compete with the shirt and the “no-thought” idea.
